Project Description

Predicting Air. Understanding Weather.

A coupled weather–chemistry forecasting system for Delhi NCR

Project Motto “Predict the Air. Understand the Weather. Act Before It’s Too Late.”

The Challenge

Air pollution in Delhi NCR isn’t just about emissions — it’s shaped by the weather. Temperature inversions, low wind speeds, and a shallow Planetary Boundary Layer (PBL) trap pollutants near the ground, while high aerosol levels can in turn alter local atmospheric conditions, creating a feedback loop between weather and air quality.

We’re building a high-resolution, coupled weather–chemistry forecasting system for Delhi NCR, using frameworks like WRF-Chem to connect meteorology with pollutants such as PM2.5, PM10, O₃, and NOx.

What Our System Does

The system delivers a 72-hour, high-resolution AQI forecast, continuously tracking:

  • PM2.5, PM10 & O₃ forecasting
  • Temperature, wind & humidity patterns
  • PBL height & inversion strength
  • Stubble-burning plume movement
  • Pollution hotspots & high-risk zones
  • 72-hour AQI severity outlook

Inversions & Stubble-Burning

During a temperature inversion, warm air sits above cool surface air like a lid, trapping pollutants below. In winter, this effect combines with agricultural stubble-burning smoke drifting in from surrounding regions. By analysing wind, stability, and PBL height together, our system explains why AQI is rising and how much of it is local versus transported pollution.

The Weather–Pollution Feedback Loop

Meteorology and pollution aren’t modeled independently — the system captures how each drives the other:

Weather → Pollution

Wind, stability, inversions, and PBL height determine how pollutants disperse or get trapped.

Pollution → Weather

Dense aerosols reduce solar radiation, altering surface heating, stability, and local circulation.

Real-Time Dashboard

A live dashboard makes the forecast easy to read and act on:

  • Inversion & PBL indicators
